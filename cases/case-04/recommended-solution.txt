Before authorizing $15 million in new capacity, the manufacturer should map the full order-to-delivery process and quantify where time is lost. Analyze machine utilization, throughput, changeover times, work-in-process inventory, supplier reliability, labor scheduling, rework, and order prioritization. Compare current output with demonstrated effective capacity to determine whether constraints arise from genuinely insufficient equipment, scheduling and process inefficiencies, or disruptions elsewhere in the production system.
Address the most binding constraints first through improved production planning, preventive maintenance, reduced changeover time, supplier coordination, and targeted staffing or automation. Pilot operational changes and use throughput, on-time delivery, lead time, scrap, and unit cost as measures. If demand forecasts remain robust and the optimized system still cannot meet service targets, prepare a staged capital investment justified by incremental capacity, expected returns, and downside demand scenarios rather than approving the full investment in advance.
