The retailer should avoid committing to across-the-board staffing cuts until it understands why profit has fallen despite higher sales. A targeted diagnostic should separate changes in gross margin from changes in operating expenses, examining results by store, region, product category, and sales channel. Key questions include whether growth is being driven by discounted products, whether merchandise and fulfillment costs have increased, whether inventory losses or returns have risen, and whether labor hours are aligned with customer traffic. Comparing high-performing and low-performing stores can identify practices worth replicating.
Based on the findings, management should prioritize interventions with the strongest verified impact: revise pricing and promotions, improve purchasing and inventory planning, reduce avoidable fulfillment costs, and redesign schedules around demand rather than simply cutting headcount. Pilot the changes in representative stores and compare them with control locations. Track gross margin, operating margin, sales per labor hour, customer service measures, and repeat visits before expanding the program.
