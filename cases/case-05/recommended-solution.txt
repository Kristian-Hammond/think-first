The firm should evaluate the $20 million AI proposal as a business-process transformation, not assume that automating tasks will automatically produce a 25% reduction in labor costs. Break down major workflows to identify tasks with sufficient volume, repeatability, and data quality for AI support, while distinguishing work that requires professional judgment, client interaction, or strict confidentiality. Run controlled pilots in research and document drafting, measuring actual time saved, output accuracy, review effort, client impact, and compliance risks. Estimate the complete cost of software, integration, training, security, and ongoing oversight.
Proceed through a phased rollout only where pilots show a defensible return and acceptable quality. Use human review for consequential outputs, clear data-use and privacy controls, staff training, and explicit accountability for AI-assisted work. Treat capacity gains as potential benefits for faster delivery, expanded service, or selective cost reduction rather than presuming immediate layoffs. Track net productivity, rework rates, client satisfaction, realization of financial benefits, adoption, and incidents, with periodic investment decisions tied to results.
