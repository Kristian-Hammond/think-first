The firm should not start with blanket discounts, which could sacrifice revenue without addressing the actual reasons customers leave. First, segment attrition by customer tenure, product, profitability, acquisition channel, and service history, and distinguish customer satisfaction from actual switching behavior. Combine account and transaction data with exit interviews, complaint analysis, and competitor comparisons to test explanations such as pricing, changing customer needs, poor onboarding, service friction, or attractive competing products.
Then develop interventions targeted to the drivers uncovered: improve onboarding and account support, resolve recurring service issues, introduce more relevant offerings, and offer retention incentives only to customer segments where they are likely to change behavior profitably. Test targeted treatments against comparable control groups. Measure retention and churn by segment, customer lifetime value, cost per retained customer, complaint resolution, and net revenue retention to verify that lower attrition creates sustainable value.
