CASE 3  /  COMPANY BRIEF
Company Profile — HarborPoint Financial
Customer Attrition at a Financial Services Firm
Company at a glance
HarborPoint Financial is a fictional regional financial-services firm offering savings, investment, and financial-planning products to individuals and small businesses. It serves customers primarily through digital channels and a network of advisers. Leadership is concerned that account closures and declining balances are accelerating despite apparently favorable satisfaction results.
Performance snapshot
Over 18 months, annualized customer attrition increased from 8% to 12%, while the latest satisfaction survey still reports 88% favorable responses. The two figures reflect different measures and populations. Management has not yet established whether exits are concentrated in particular customer groups, product types, tenures, or acquisition channels.
Operating model
Customer service is centralized, but advisers control many individual client relationships. Product and pricing teams set rates and fees; marketing manages acquisition campaigns. Data sits in multiple systems, making it difficult to connect satisfaction responses, usage, balances, costs, and individual reasons for departure.
Leadership perspectives
The commercial director believes competitors’ lower prices explain the losses and favors broad discounts. The finance director worries that indiscriminate incentives would reduce earnings without changing long-term loyalty. Advisers report that some customers are consolidating accounts or moving to more convenient digital offerings.
Decision context
The executive team needs a retention strategy before the next planning cycle. It can consider targeted pricing, service or product changes, and new engagement programs, but wants a credible estimate of incremental retained value and a way to test what works before committing to large-scale discounts.
