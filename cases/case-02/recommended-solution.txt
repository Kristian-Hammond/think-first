The company should reject a simultaneous five-country launch and instead develop a ranked market-entry plan. Evaluate candidate countries on addressable demand, competition, buyer willingness to pay, language and localization effort, data protection and contracting requirements, channel access, and expected customer acquisition costs. Validate the highest-ranked markets with interviews, competitor analysis, and small-scale demand tests. Choose one or two initial countries where the product has a credible advantage and the company can support customers effectively.
Use a staged portion of the $10 million budget for localized product changes, legal and tax setup, limited commercial staffing, and a focused pilot; preserve the balance for contingencies and follow-on expansion. Establish decision gates based on qualified pipeline, conversion, acquisition cost, retention, support burden, and contribution margin. Expand only after the initial markets demonstrate repeatable economics and operational readiness, adjusting the product and go-to-market approach to local evidence rather than assuming U.S. success will transfer unchanged.
